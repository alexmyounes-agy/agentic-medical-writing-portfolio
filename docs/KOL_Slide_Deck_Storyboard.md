## Slide 1: Clinical Landscape & The Unmet Need

### Action Headline
> **First-Line Platinum-Based Chemotherapy Offers Limited Long-Term Survival in Advanced NSCLC, Underscoring the Urgent Need for Biomarker-Driven Targeted Regimens**

### Visual / Layout Blueprint
* **Layout:** Two-column split layout.
* **Left Column:** 3 concise bullet points outlining the clinical status quo, historical survival benchmarks, and limitations of cytotoxic duos.
* **Right Column:** High-impact callout card highlighting historical 5-year overall survival (<5%) and typical median PFS under platinum-doublet therapy (4–6 months).
* **Footer:** Formal clinical reference citation.

### Slide Body Copy
* **High Disease Burden:** Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounting for ~85% of diagnoses—predominantly presenting with advanced or metastatic disease.
* **Ceiling of Cytotoxic Chemotherapy:** 
  * Platinum-doublet regimens (e.g., carboplatin/cisplatin + pemetrexed/gemcitabine) have represented the historical standard of care for over a decade.
  * Median progression-free survival (PFS) remains restricted to 4–6 months, with median overall survival (OS) plateauing at approximately 10–12 months.
* **Severe Tolerability Constraints:** High rates of cumulative systemic toxicities (myelosuppression, neuropathy, nephrotoxicity, intractable nausea) frequently force dose reductions or premature discontinuation.
* **The Precision Gap:** While patients harboring sensitizing *EGFR* mutations or *ALK* translocations benefit from targeted TKIs, the vast majority (~80–85%) of patients lack actionable oncogenic drivers and require novel mechanistic approaches.

### Verbatim Speaker Notes (Medical Science Liaison / Presenter Script)
> *"Good morning, colleagues. To frame our discussion today, let us examine the frontline landscape of advanced non-small-cell lung cancer prior to the arrival of checkpoint blockade. For over fifteen years, medical oncology has operated against a stubborn therapeutic ceiling. Platinum-based doublet chemotherapy has remained our default frontline standard of care, yet it delivers a median progression-free survival of barely four to six months, with five-year overall survival hovering under five percent.*
>
> *While precision oncology has transformed care for patients with targetable EGFR mutations or ALK rearrangements, roughly eighty-five percent of our frontline advanced NSCLC population present without actionable driver alterations. For this massive majority, cytotoxic chemotherapy has been an aggressive, non-specific compromise: high rates of grade 3 and 4 myelosuppression, frequent treatment holidays, and predictable disease progression within half a year. The clinical imperative was unmistakable: we needed an intervention that could generate deep, durable anti-tumour responses without the systemic destruction of cytotoxic agents."*

## Slide 2: Biological Mechanism: The PD-1/PD-L1 Axis

### Action Headline
> **Tumour-Mediated PD-L1 Upregulation Subverts Host Immune Surveillance; Pembrolizumab Reactivates Pre-Existing T-Cell Anti-Tumour Immunity**

### Visual / Layout Blueprint
* **Layout:** Top half = schematic molecular mechanism of action diagram. Bottom half = 3 horizontal thematic callout cards.
* **Diagram Elements:**
  * *Panel A (Immune Evasion):* Tumour cell expressing PD-L1 binding to PD-1 receptor on CD8+ cytotoxic T-cell, delivering an inhibitory checkpoint signal that leads to T-cell exhaustion and anergy.
  * *Panel B (Immune Reactivation):* Pembrolizumab (high-affinity, humanized IgG4-kappa monoclonal antibody) selectively binding PD-1, blocking interaction with PD-L1 and PD-L2, relieving inhibition, and triggering T-cell proliferation and cytolytic granule release (perforin/granzyme).
* **Bottom Cards:** (1) Receptor Affinity; (2) Antibody Engineering (IgG4 avoids ADCC); (3) Biomarker Expression (PD-L1 as an adaptive immune resistance marker).

### Slide Body Copy
* **Mechanism of Immune Evasion:** Tumour cells exploit physiological immune checkpoints by upregulating Programmed Death-Ligand 1 (PD-L1), engaging PD-1 receptors on activated CD8+ T-lymphocytes to induce peripheral immune tolerance and effector exhaustion.
* **Targeted Checkpoint Inhibition:**
  * **Agent:** Pembrolizumab is a potent, humanized monoclonal IgG4-kappa isotype antibody engineered against the human PD-1 receptor.
  * **Dual Ligand Blockade:** Sterically hinders the interaction of PD-1 with both PD-L1 and PD-L2 without triggering antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) or complement fixation.
* **Restoring Cytolytic Capacity:** Checkpoint disinhibition restores antigen-specific T-cell effector functions, promoting sustained tumour clearance and immunological memory.
* **The Biomarker Hypothesis:** High tumour cell surface expression of PD-L1 (TPS $\ge 50\%$) identifies tumours actively engaged in adaptive immune resistance, creating a primed substrate for checkpoint blockade.

### Verbatim Speaker Notes
> *"Here we examine the molecular rationale underpinning this paradigm shift. Tumour immune evasion is not merely passive camouflage; it is an active, receptor-mediated shutdown of our immune defenses. Tumour cells upregulate PD-L1 in response to endogenous interferon-gamma, engaging the PD-1 receptor on our tumour-infiltrating CD8+ T-cells. This interaction transmits a potent inhibitory signal that paralyzes the T-cell, inducing exhaustion and clonal anergy.*
>
> *Pembrolizumab is a highly specific, humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibody designed to intercept this interaction. Because it is engineered with an IgG4 backbone, it avoids complement activation or antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity against our own T-cells. Instead, it simply caps the PD-1 receptor, preventing both PD-L1 and PD-L2 from docking. The consequence is immediate disinhibition: the exhausted T-cell recovers its proliferative capacity, releases perforin and granzymes, and mounts an authentic host immune response against the neoplasm. Crucially, the KEYNOTE investigators hypothesized that patients whose tumours express high levels of PD-L1 are the very cohort whose immune systems are primed and waiting for this checkpoint lock to be broken."*

## Slide 3: KEYNOTE-024 Study Design & Patient Selection

### Action Headline
> **KEYNOTE-024 Evaluated Pembrolizumab Monotherapy vs. Standard Platinum-Doublet Chemotherapy in Biomarker-Enriched, Treatment-Naïve Advanced NSCLC**

### Visual / Layout Blueprint
* **Layout:** Classical clinical trial schema flowchart (Left-to-Right) with a dedicated Biomarker Strategy callout box on the bottom right.
* **Flowchart Steps:**
  1. *Key Eligibility Box:* Advanced/metastatic (Stage IV) NSCLC; Treatment-naïve; No sensitizing *EGFR* / *ALK* alterations; ECOG PS 0–1; Central laboratory evaluation of PD-L1 expression.
  2. *Biomarker Gate Box:* PD-L1 Tumour Proportion Score (TPS) $\ge 50\%$ (PD-L1 IHC 22C3 pharmDx).
  3. *1:1 Randomisation Arrow (N = 305):*
     * **Arm A (n = 154):** Pembrolizumab 200 mg fixed dose IV every 3 weeks (up to 35 cycles / 2 years) or until progression/unacceptable toxicity.
     * **Arm B (n = 151):** Investigator's choice platinum-doublet chemotherapy (carboplatin or cisplatin + pemetrexed [non-squamous], gemcitabine [squamous], or paclitaxel) for 4–6 cycles + optional pemetrexed maintenance.
  4. *Cross-Over Box:* Explicit cross-over to pembrolizumab permitted upon verified disease progression in the chemotherapy arm.
* **Biomarker Enrichment Callout Card:**
  * *Why TPS $\ge 50\%$?* Derived from KEYNOTE-001 data identifying $\ge 50\%$ as the decisive inflection threshold where monotherapy ORR tripled (~45% vs ~15%).
  * *Clinical Prevalence:* 30.2% of frontline screened patients fall into this hyper-responsive bracket.
* **Endpoints Box:** Primary: PFS (Blinded Independent Central Review per RECIST v1.1); Secondary: OS, ORR, Safety.

### Slide Body Copy
* **Strategic Biomarker Enrichment (TPS $\ge 50\%$):**
  * Screened 1,945 patients; 1,653 had evaluable tissue for PD-L1 expression via the validated companion diagnostic IHC 22C3 pharmDx assay.
  * **500 patients (30.2%)** demonstrated high PD-L1 expression (TPS $\ge 50\%$), establishing that nearly one-third of advanced NSCLC patients meet this biomarker-defined enrichment threshold.
  * *Rationale:* Prior Phase I/II KEYNOTE-001 findings demonstrated that TPS $\ge 50\%$ marked the biological inflection point where single-agent PD-1 blockade achieved maximal antitumour activity, ensuring optimal probability of outperforming platinum-based chemotherapy.
* **Stratification Factors:** ECOG Performance Status (0 vs. 1), Histology (Squamous vs. Non-squamous), Geographic Region (East Asia vs. Non-East Asia).
* **Ethical Cross-Over Design:** Patients in the chemotherapy arm with verified objective disease progression were permitted to cross over to receive second-line pembrolizumab monotherapy (**43.7% in-study cross-over rate**).

### Verbatim Speaker Notes
> *"Let us turn to the study design of KEYNOTE-024. This was a multinational, open-label, randomized Phase III study that enrolled 305 treatment-naïve patients with Stage IV NSCLC. The pivotal strategic feature of this trial is its biomarker enrichment.*
>
> *Clinicians often ask: why did the investigators settle on a PD-L1 cutoff of fifty percent or higher, rather than a lower threshold like one percent? The answer lies in the early-phase KEYNOTE-001 trial. In that study, fifty percent represented a clear biological inflection point: patients with a TPS under fifty percent achieved response rates of fifteen to twenty percent, whereas those with a TPS of fifty percent or greater achieved response rates exceeding forty-five percent. The investigators deliberately chose this hyper-enriched cohort to test the bold hypothesis: can single-agent immunotherapy truly dethrone multi-agent cytotoxic chemotherapy as a frontline standard?*
>
> *Investigators screened nearly two thousand patients using the companion diagnostic IHC 22C3 assay. Exactly 30.2% of evaluable patients met this TPS fifty percent threshold—confirming that this is not a niche subpopulation, but roughly one in every three advanced NSCLC patients who walk into our clinics. Patients with EGFR mutations or ALK translocations were excluded, and the remaining cohort was randomized one-to-one to either flat-dose pembrolizumab at 200 mg every three weeks, or platinum-doublet chemotherapy with optional pemetrexed maintenance.*
>
> *Crucially, the protocol allowed ethical cross-over upon confirmed progression. Nearly half the chemotherapy cohort crossed over to pembrolizumab on-study, which makes the resulting survival advantages all the more profound."*

## Slide 4: Primary Endpoint: Progression-Free Survival (PFS)

### Action Headline
> **Pembrolizumab Monotherapy Demonstrated a 50% Reduction in the Risk of Disease Progression or Death, Doubling Median PFS to 10.3 Months**

### Visual / Layout Blueprint
* **Layout:** Dominant Kaplan-Meier curve graphic on the left (0 to 18 months); Callout summary card and subgroup forest plot highlight on the right.
* **KM Curve Visuals:**
  * Blue curve (Pembrolizumab) showing early and sustained separation starting at the first 9-week scan.
  * Grey curve (Chemotherapy) showing steep early progression.
  * Hazard Ratio bracket: **HR = 0.50 (95% CI, 0.37–0.68); P < 0.001**.
* **Key Numbers Card:**
  * Median PFS: **10.3 months** (95% CI, 6.7 to NR) vs. **6.0 months** (95% CI, 4.2 to 6.2).
  * 6-Month PFS Rate: **62.1%** vs. **50.3%**.
  * 12-Month PFS Rate: **48.0%** vs. **21.2%** (more than double at 1 year).

### Slide Body Copy
* **Statistically Significant & Clinically Meaningful:** Primary endpoint was met at the pre-specified second interim analysis, leading the Independent Data Monitoring Committee (IDMC) to recommend early trial unblinding.
* **Halving the Hazard Rate:** Hazard ratio of **0.50** represents an unprecedented 50% reduction in the risk of progression or death for pembrolizumab vs. chemotherapy.
* **Durable Plateau:** Separation of curves emerges at first assessment (9 weeks) and widens over time; at 12 months, nearly half (48.0%) of pembrolizumab patients remained progression-free vs. only 21.2% in the chemotherapy arm.
* **Consistent Across Subgroups:** PFS benefit favoured pembrolizumab across all pre-specified subgroups, including age (<65 vs $\ge 65$), sex, ECOG status (0 vs 1), and histology (squamous vs non-squamous).

### Verbatim Speaker Notes
> *"Slide 4 presents the primary endpoint: progression-free survival assessed by blinded independent central review. The results are decisive. Median progression-free survival was 10.3 months with pembrolizumab versus 6.0 months with platinum-doublet chemotherapy. The hazard ratio was 0.50, with a p-value of less than 0.001.*
>
> *What is particularly striking about this Kaplan-Meier curve is not just the 4.3-month absolute extension in median PFS, but the trajectory of the curves. Notice how early they separate—at the first restaging scan at week nine—and how the gap widens over time. By month twelve, the progression-free rate on chemotherapy drops to 21%, whereas nearly half—48%—of patients on pembrolizumab remain free of disease progression. When you examine the forest plot across every pre-specified baseline subgroup, the benefit consistently falls to the left of unity. In a disease where PFS gains are typically measured in weeks, doubling median PFS represents a profound clinical advance."*

## Slide 5: Secondary Efficacy: Overall Survival (OS)

### Action Headline
> **First-Line Pembrolizumab Conferred a Significant 40% Reduction in Mortality Despite Substantial Control-Arm Cross-Over to Immunotherapy**

### Visual / Layout Blueprint
* **Layout:** Left = Kaplan-Meier Overall Survival Curve; Right = Two data callouts + Dedicated Cross-Over Sensitivity callout box.
* **KM Curve Visuals:**
  * Pembrolizumab curve tracking consistently above chemotherapy from month 2 onwards.
  * HR for death: **0.60 (95% CI, 0.41–0.89); P = 0.005**.
* **Key Stats Callout:**
  * 6-Month Overall Survival Rate: **80.2%** (pembro) vs. **72.4%** (chemo).
  * 12-Month Overall Survival Rate: **70.3%** (pembro) vs. **54.0%** (chemo).
* **Cross-Over Sensitivity Callout Card:**
  * *On-Study Cross-Over:* 43.7% (66/151 chemotherapy patients crossed over to pembrolizumab upon progression).
  * *Effective Total Cross-Over:* >50% (additional 7.9% received non-study anti-PD-[L]1 agents).
  * *Statistical Adjustment (RPSFT Model):* Adjusting for cross-over dilution yields an estimated un-confounded HR of **~0.49**, demonstrating that standard ITT analysis conservatively underestimates the true survival magnitude.

### Slide Body Copy
* **Statistically Robust Survival Superiority:** Despite an early interim analysis window (median follow-up of 11.2 months), frontline pembrolizumab monotherapy conferred a statistically significant 40% reduction in mortality risk (HR = 0.60; P = 0.005).
* **The Cross-Over Dilution Phenomenon:**
  * In standard Intention-to-Treat (ITT) trial design, allowing control-arm patients to receive active therapy upon progression creates a positive survival drag on the control arm, compressing curve separation.
  * Over 50% of chemotherapy patients effectively crossed over to second-line checkpoint blockade (43.7% on-study, 7.9% off-study).
  * That an OS benefit remained statistically significant under such high cross-over confirms the decisive advantage of frontline vs. second-line timing.
* **Early Study Termination on Ethical Grounds:** Recognizing that delaying checkpoint blockade compromised patient survival, the Independent Data Monitoring Committee (IDMC) unanimously recommended terminating the trial early to permit all control patients immediate cross-over access.

### Verbatim Speaker Notes
> *"Slide 5 highlights the secondary endpoint that clinicians and payers scrutinize most: overall survival. In modern clinical trial methodology, demonstrating a definitive overall survival advantage in the frontline metastatic setting is notoriously difficult, primarily because ethical considerations mandate permitting cross-over upon progression.*
>
> *Let us look closely at the control arm of KEYNOTE-024. Forty-three point seven percent of patients randomized to chemotherapy crossed over to pembrolizumab on-study upon disease progression, and another eight percent received off-study PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitors. In total, more than half of the chemotherapy cohort received checkpoint blockade second-line. In an intention-to-treat analysis, this creates substantial 'cross-over dilution'—the survival gains achieved by control patients when they receive salvage immunotherapy artificially elevate the chemotherapy curve and compress the hazard ratio.*
>
> *Yet, even against this diluted baseline, frontline pembrolizumab monotherapy produced a statistically significant forty percent reduction in the risk of death, with an intention-to-treat hazard ratio of 0.60 and a p-value of 0.005. When biostatisticians apply counterfactual adjustment models—such as the Rank-Preserving Structural Failure Time (RPSFT) method—to account for cross-over, the estimated un-confounded hazard ratio drops to approximately 0.49.*
>
> *The clinical takeaway is undeniable: you cannot simply give chemotherapy first and expect to 'catch up' by offering immunotherapy second-line. Initiating checkpoint blockade immediately in the frontline setting preserves organ function, prevents early fatal progressions, and delivers a survival foundation that delayed therapy cannot replicate."*

## Slide 6: Secondary Efficacy: Response Rate & Duration

### Action Headline
> **Pembrolizumab Significantly Increased Objective Responses (44.8% vs. 27.8%) and Delivered Unprecedented, Sustained Durations of Response**

### Visual / Layout Blueprint
* **Layout:** Side-by-side comparative graphics.
* **Left Panel:** Grouped bar chart comparing Objective Response Rate (ORR: 44.8% vs. 27.8%, P = 0.0011), Complete Response (CR: 3.9% vs. 0.7%), and Partial Response (PR: 40.9% vs. 27.2%).
* **Right Panel:** Duration of Response (DOR) swimmer plot / timeline callout:
  * Pembrolizumab median DOR: **Not Reached** (range 1.9+ to 14.5+ months).
  * Chemotherapy median DOR: **6.3 months** (range 2.1+ to 12.6+ months).
  * Responders remaining in response: **88%** (pembro) vs. **54%** (chemo).

### Slide Body Copy
* **Higher Antitumour Efficacy:**
  * ORR was **44.8%** (95% CI, 36.8–53.0) with pembrolizumab vs. **27.8%** (95% CI, 20.8–35.7) with platinum chemotherapy, representing an absolute improvement of +17.0%.
* **Rapid Onset of Action:**
  * Median time to response was identical in both arms at **2.2 months**, countering historical misconceptions that immunotherapy exhibits delayed clinical response onset compared to chemotherapy.
* **The Hallmark of Immunological Memory (DOR):**
  * Median duration of response was **not reached** in the pembrolizumab group, with 88% of responding patients maintaining response at the time of data cutoff.
  * In contrast, responses to chemotherapy were characteristically transient, with a median DOR of only 6.3 months.

### Verbatim Speaker Notes
> *"Slide 6 addresses two critical clinical questions: how often do tumours shrink, and how long does that shrinkage last? First, look at the Objective Response Rate on the left: nearly forty-five percent of patients treated with single-agent pembrolizumab achieved confirmed objective response, compared to twenty-eight percent on chemotherapy. Complete responses were observed in six patients on pembrolizumab versus only one on chemotherapy.*
>
> *Second, note the kinetics of response. The median time to response was 2.2 months in both arms, demonstrating that when immunotherapy works, it acts just as rapidly as cytotoxic regimens. But the true distinction lies in the right panel: duration of response. Chemotherapy produces temporary shrinkage; the median duration was just 6.3 months, and nearly half of responders had already progressed by cutoff. In the pembrolizumab arm, median duration of response was not reached—eighty-eight percent of responding patients remained in ongoing response. This persistence of response is the tangible hallmark of immunological memory."*

## Slide 7: Safety & Tolerability: Favourable Toxicity Profile & Active imAE Management

### Action Headline
> **Pembrolizumab Halved Severe High-Grade Toxicities Compared to Chemotherapy, Presenting a Distinct but Algorithmic and Reversible Immune Safety Profile**

### Visual / Layout Blueprint
* **Layout:** 3-Part Safety Dashboard with Top Metric Badges, Bottom Comparative Table, and Right-Hand "Clinical Reassurance & Management Algorithm" Panel.
* **Top Metric Badges:**
  * Any-Grade TRAEs: **73.4%** (pembro) vs. **90.0%** (chemo)
  * Grade 3–5 Severe TRAEs: **26.6%** (pembro) vs. **53.3%** (chemo) [**>50% Relative Reduction**]
  * Discontinuation for TRAEs: **7.1%** (pembro) vs. **10.7%** (chemo)
* **Bottom Comparative Table:**
  * Cytotoxic vs. Immune Tolerability: Nausea (10.4% vs 43.3%), Fatigue (10.4% vs 28.7%), Anemia (5.2% vs 34.0%), Neutropenia (0% vs 13.3%), Alopecia (0% vs ~30%).
* **Right Panel — Algorithmic imAE Management Protocol:**
  * *Pneumonitis Breakdown:* 5.8% all-grade (9/154 patients); only 2.6% Grade 3–5 (4/154 patients); 0 treatment-related deaths.
  * *Grade 1 (Asymptomatic / Radiographic):* Continue or withhold; repeat CT scan in 2–3 weeks.
  * *Grade 2 (Symptomatic, mild/moderate dyspnea/cough):* Withhold pembrolizumab; initiate oral prednisone (1–2 mg/kg/day); taper slowly over $\ge 4$ weeks upon symptom resolution to Grade $\le 1$.
  * *Grade 3–4 (Severe hypoxia / life-threatening):* Permanently discontinue; urgent hospitalization; IV methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg/day; add infliximab/mycophenolate if refractory at 48 hours.

### Slide Body Copy
* **Substantial Reduction in High-Grade Toxicities:**
  * Treatment-related Grade 3–5 adverse events were reduced by more than half (**26.6% vs. 53.3%**), driven by the complete elimination of chemotherapy-induced myelosuppression and febrile neutropenia.
  * Overall treatment discontinuation due to adverse events was lower with pembrolizumab (**7.1% vs. 10.7%**).
* **Immune-Mediated Adverse Events (imAEs) — Expected & Treatable:**
  * Total imAE incidence was **29.2%** (pembro) vs. 4.7% (chemo); severe Grade 3/4 imAEs occurred in **9.7%** of patients.
  * Most frequent: Hypothyroidism (9.1%), Hyperthyroidism (7.8%), Pneumonitis (5.8%), Severe skin reactions (3.9%).
  * Endocrinopathies are easily managed with routine hormone replacement (e.g. levothyroxine) without treatment interruption.
* **Proactive Clinical Management of Pneumonitis:**
  * Grade 3–5 pneumonitis occurred in only 4 patients (2.6%); no treatment-related deaths occurred in the pembrolizumab arm (vs. 3 fatal treatment-related events on chemotherapy).
  * Clinical vigilance (educating patients on early dry cough and dyspnea) paired with early HRCT imaging and standardized steroid tapering results in rapid, complete symptom resolution in the vast majority of cases without blunting anti-tumour efficacy.

### Verbatim Speaker Notes
> *"Slide 7 covers the safety profile, and I want to spend a moment directly addressing the clinical questions that thoracic oncologists and pulmonologists inevitably raise when evaluating first-line immunotherapy: how do we manage immune-mediated toxicities, and specifically, what about pneumonitis?*
>
> *First, let us appreciate the macroscopic picture: pembrolizumab fundamentally transforms the patient experience. The debilitating, systemic toxicities of platinum-doublet chemotherapy—the neutropenic sepsis, profound anemia, severe nausea, and alopecia—are virtually eliminated. Any-grade toxicities dropped from 90% to 73%, and severe grade 3 to 5 toxicities were cut cleanly in half: 26.6% on pembrolizumab versus 53.3% on chemotherapy. Discontinuations due to toxicity were lower on pembrolizumab, at 7.1% compared to nearly 11% on chemotherapy.*
>
> *However, immunotherapy introduces a distinct spectrum of immune-related events that requires our multidisciplinary teams to adopt a new management playbook. Overall, immune-mediated toxicities occurred in twenty-nine percent of patients, but high-grade events were restricted to under ten percent. Thyroid dysfunction—seen in roughly nine percent—is straightforward: asymptomatic TSH monitoring, prompt initiation of levothyroxine, and continuation of pembrolizumab.*
>
> *Now, let us tackle pneumonitis directly. Across the entire trial, pneumonitis occurred in 5.8% of patients, and severe grade 3 or higher events occurred in only 2.6%—exactly four patients. There were zero treatment-related deaths in the pembrolizumab arm, compared to three toxic deaths on chemotherapy. The key to clinical success is not avoiding immunotherapy out of fear of pneumonitis, but establishing an aggressive, algorithmic response protocol.*
>
> *We educate patients upfront to report any new or worsening dry cough or shortness of breath. When a patient presents with mild, Grade 2 symptoms, our algorithm is clear: rule out pulmonary infection with sputum cultures and high-resolution CT, withhold pembrolizumab immediately, and initiate oral prednisone at one to two milligrams per kilogram per day. The critical clinical pearl is the taper: steroids must be tapered slowly over at least four weeks once symptoms resolve to Grade 1 to prevent inflammatory rebound. For rare Grade 3 or 4 presentations, patients are hospitalized, switched to IV methylprednisolone, and pembrolizumab is permanently discontinued.*
>
> *Real-world data and subsequent trial follow-ups have confirmed that with prompt algorithmic steroid intervention, the overwhelming majority of immune pneumonitis cases resolve completely, and importantly, systemic steroid administration for toxicity does not negate the durable anti-tumour efficacy established by checkpoint blockade. This makes the risk-benefit equation decisively favourable for our patients."*
